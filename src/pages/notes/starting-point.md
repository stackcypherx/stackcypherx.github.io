## Where this starts

Zero professional experience in security, networking, or AI. A structured 100-day TryHackMe plan in
hand, a home lab to build, and 24 months to become a credible candidate in the global market.

The plan itself lives on the [roadmap](/roadmap). This note exists to state the commitments, because
a plan nobody has seen is a plan you can quietly abandon in March.

## The rules

**One artifact per milestone.** No checkbox gets ticked on this site without a link. Not a course
completion screenshot — a repo, a writeup, a badge, a report, a merged PR. If I cannot produce the
link, the work is not finished.

**One writeup a week, in English, published whether or not it feels ready.** Fifty-two a year. This
is the only part of the plan that compounds, and it is the part that is easiest to skip because
nobody notices immediately when it stops.

**One primary track, not three.** Cybersecurity, at roughly 70% of hours. Networks and AI at 15%
each — there to make me unusual, not to make me employable. Revisited in six months, not six days.

**One certification per quarter, maximum**, and each one has to unlock a filter on a job posting I
have actually read. No posting, no exam.

**A minimum viable week I never drop below**: one lab and one paragraph. Not the target — the floor.
Most people who stop, stop somewhere around month seven, and the defence against that is a floor low
enough to clear on a bad week.

## What "done" looks like in 24 months

- A practical, pass/fail security credential — OSCP or PNPT
- Two flagship projects with live URLs, one of them in the AI × security overlap
- Fifty-plus published writeups
- Three recommendation letters from three different organisations
- One conference or meetup talk delivered
- Either an offer, or a specific and diagnosed reason there isn't one

## Why publish this

Two reasons. The first is accountability that costs nothing and works anyway. The second is that
recognition — the kind the strongest visa routes actually require as evidence — is not something you
can assemble in the month you need it. It is a byproduct of having been visibly useful for a long
time. Starting the clock today is free.

The next entry will be a real technical writeup. This one is just the line in the sand.
